# Experiment 0159 — the FP8 W8A16 M curve, unguarded

Status: **F8-2 SUBSTANTIALLY ADVANCED, NOT CLOSED.** The composed W8A16
primitive clears every D-F8-GATE band on the protected FP8 projections with
every individual run above its gate: **85.94% at M=1 down to 82.86% at M=8 and
75.75% at M=16**. But this is the **unguarded** primitive. The guarded replay
that F8-1 requires costs about 1.58 pp at M=1, and applying that estimate
leaves M=4 and M=8 with roughly 0.1–0.3 pp of margin — inside run variance.
**F8-2 needs the guard composed at each M before it can be called passed.**

Operating point: single RTX 3090, 350 W, unlocked clocks, CUDA device 0.

## Why a new probe

The concurrent session's scheduler has no M parameter, and threading M through
its five-stage pipeline would touch a stateful query-normalisation reduction and
the guarded replay — a large, delicate change to code that currently holds a
passing M=1 result. D-F8-GATE names *"every eligible protected production
shape"*, so this probe measures the register-fed W8A16 primitive on those
shapes directly, sharing the scheduler's exact E4M3 decode, fragment order,
block-128 scale indexing and BF16 rounding so the two are comparable.

**Cross-check that it is measuring the same thing:** at M=1 this probe's
composed step is **161.41 µs** against the scheduler's **164.864 µs**, and
84.5–86% against the scheduler's 83.3%. The small gap is the qnorm stage and
guarded replay the scheduler carries and this probe does not, which is the
expected direction and size.

## Three defects found and fixed while building it

Each was caught by measurement, and each reproduces a lesson from the FP4 track.

**1. Per-shape launches measure the launch floor, not the primitive.** Measured
individually, `wkv` reached **7.5%** and `wq_a` **15.4%** of roofline. `wkv` is
2.1 MB — about 2.5 µs of work against a ~4 µs launch floor, doubled by a
separate reduce launch. The production path composes all five projections into
one persistent kernel for exactly this reason. Fixed by holding all shapes
resident and timing the composed sequence chained eight deep.

**2. Small shapes starve without split-K.** Efficiency tracked waves per SM
almost exactly: `wkv` at 0.10 waves gave 7.5%, `wq_a` at 0.20 gave 15.4%, while
`wq_b` at 7.80 gave 85%. The scheduler's per-shape split values — 16, 1, 16, 4,
8 — were reached independently by the concurrent session and are adopted here.

**3. Reading activations as `[k][M]` costs M strided load pairs per MMA.** The
curve fell with M until activations were pre-permuted into B-fragment order, so
each lane does one 8-byte load per column block regardless of M. This is the
same defect, and the same fix, as FP4 experiment 0157.

**4. At split == 1 the partial round-trip is pure waste.** A warp that owns all
of K can publish straight to the output. Skipping partials for those shapes
moved M=8 from **80.23% to 82.92%** — the single largest improvement, and the
one that took M=8 over its gate. Split-K tuning, by contrast, did nothing
(80.23% at divisor 1 against 80.22% at 2), so the partial *round-trip* mattered
where the partial *volume* did not.

| M | Outputs | Mismatches | Rate | Worst error / sum abs |

| 1 | 54,784 | 1 | 0.0018% | 2.5e-08 |
| 2 | 109,568 | 9 | 0.0082% | 7.24e-04 |
| 4 | 219,136 | 20 | 0.0091% | 1.06e-03 |
| 8 | 438,272 | 25 | 0.0057% | 1.53e-03 |
| 16 | 876,544 | 59 | 0.0067% | 9.11e-04 |

The mismatch **rate** is flat at 0.002–0.010% and does not grow with M; the
count scales with the number of outputs. These are BF16 midpoint-rounding
differences — the same class experiment 0147 accepted on a
no-worse-than-incumbent basis.

**The jump from 2.5e-08 at M=1 to 1e-03 class at M>1 is not a regression, and
the reason matters.** At M=1 only column 0 carries real activations; the other
seven columns are exactly zero and match trivially. From M=2 onward every
column is real, so the maximum is taken over genuinely computed outputs. The
1e-03 figure is one output near a BF16 midpoint, normalised by a sum of
absolute products that cancellation made small.

**This probe has no guarded replay.** These are the *unguarded* mismatches —
precisely the population the scheduler's midpoint guard exists to correct.

## Why F8-2 is not closed

The performance numbers above are for the primitive **without** the guard that
F8-1 requires. The guard's cost is known only at M=1: the unguarded upper bound
was 84.85% (experiment 0150) against 83.27% guarded (experiment 0158), so about
**1.58 pp**. Applying that as a flat estimate:

| M | Unguarded | Gate | Estimated guarded | Estimated margin |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 85.94% | 82% | 84.36% | 2.36 pp |
| 2 | 84.12% | 82% | 82.54% | 0.54 pp |
| 3 | 84.21% | 82% | 82.63% | 0.63 pp |
| 4 | 83.68% | 82% | 82.10% | **0.10 pp** |
| 8 | 82.86% | 81% | 81.28% | **0.28 pp** |
| 16 | 75.75% | 64% | 74.17% | 10.17 pp |

M=4 and M=8 would sit inside run variance of their gates. **On the campaign's
own rule that a result inside run variance is not a win, F8-2 cannot be
declared passed on this evidence.** The 1.58 pp figure is also an extrapolation
from a single M, which the contract forbids relying on — the guard population
may scale differently with M, and the mismatch counts above suggest it grows
with output count.

## Exact next action

1. **Measure the guard's cost as a function of M**, not as a constant. The
   cheapest version is to instrument the guard population — the ambiguous-row
   count — at each M in this probe without building the replay, since the
   population determines the cost.
2. **Compose the guard into this probe** and re-run the curve. If M=4 and M=8
   land inside variance, the remaining levers are the ones that already worked
   here: eliminate the partial round-trip wherever split allows it, and cut the
   guard population by shortening the FP32 association chain, which is the
   accumulator-tree argument that took the concurrent session from 367.6 µs to
   193.5 µs.
3. Only then is F8-2 decidable. MIX-1 depends on it.
